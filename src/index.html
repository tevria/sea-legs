Help

Quick fixes

  • Eyes on the horizonGet outside if you can and look at the horizon or the shoreline. It beats any screen.
  • Midships and lowMotion is usually gentlest near the middle of the ship and on lower decks.
  • Fresh airFace into the breeze. Stay clear of exhaust and strong food smells.
  • Small, plain snacksCrackers or dry toast. Some people feel worse on an empty stomach.
  • GingerSome people find ginger tea or chews settle their stomach.
  • Wrist pressure pointSome people find firm pressure helps: three finger widths below the wrist crease, between the two tendons.
  • Sip waterSmall sips, often. Go easy on alcohol and heavy meals.
  • Lie down, eyes closedIf it gets bad, lying still with your eyes closed can help.
Medication

There are over-the-counter and prescription options. Most work best taken before you sail, and some cause drowsiness. Ask a pharmacist or doctor which is right for you.

When to get help

See the ship's medical centre or a doctor if you're vomiting repeatedly, can't keep fluids down, feel faint or confused, have chest pain or trouble breathing, or if symptoms carry on after you're back on land.
